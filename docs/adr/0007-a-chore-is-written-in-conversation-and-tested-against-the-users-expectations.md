# 0007 - A chore is written in conversation and tested against the user's own expectations

Status: accepted
Date: 2026-10-28

## Context

The four chores in `playbooks/` were written by hand ([ADR 0005](0005-chores-are-data-rehearsed-on-a-bench.md)). A person with a need none of them covers had no way in. [Chore onboarding v2](../concepts/chore-onboarding-v2/README.md) designs the way in: the person describes the need in the main window, Athena asks one question at a time while a sketch of the process draws itself, then writes a real chore, previews the cards she expects to file, and runs a test that the person judges. Two things must not happen. A model must not decide that the conversation is complete, and Athena must not write her own answer key.

## Decision

1. **A draft is a Project from its first frame.** `POST /drafts` creates the Project and the draft, and makes the Project active. Every stage reply is an ordinary turn in that Project's conversation (surface `design`), so her small window shows the history with no new code. A draft walks `describe -> shape -> author -> preview -> rehearse -> verdict -> saved`. `shape` is re-entered from `author` (a failure that cannot be repaired), from `preview` and from a `revise` verdict.
2. **The sketch is the person's picture of the process, not the chore's source.** Shaping changes only the sketch, through two AUTO actions, `sketch` and `ask`, which are offered only on a design turn. Called anywhere else they do nothing. Athena writes the chore files in one step after the sketch is agreed.
3. **The flow owns completion.** The seven slots are `need`, `apps`, `inputs`, `steps`, `hand`, `output` and `never`. Coverage is computed from the sketch: 1 when a slot holds a confirmed node, 0.5 when it holds only Athena's proposals, 0 when empty, or `skipped` with a stated default. The draft is agreed when `need`, `apps`, `hand` and `output` are at 1 and the rest are at least 0.5 or skipped. A slot gets at most `FOLLOW_UPS_MAX` follow-up questions, and "That's enough, write it" is offered at any coverage.
4. **The person's marks are the answer key.** The preview lists the cards Athena expects to file. The person's `yes` makes a card eligible, `no` makes it a trap, and a "she must never..." line makes it forbidden. A card the person says she missed is placed on a practice-app tool and key when Athena can; one she cannot place is kept and shown, not scored. `truth.json` is written from the key alone.
5. **The test run is the bench, in process.** The bench runner plays the written chore's phases on a throwaway daemon, streams phases and cards, writes ledger rows with surface `rehearsal`, approves nothing, and is never retried automatically.
6. **Personal chores never reach the repository.** Drafts live in `<data_dir>/drafts/<draft_id>/`. A kept chore moves to `<data_dir>/chores/<chore_id>/` and is listed beside the library with `origin: "yours"`. Erasing a Project erases its draft.

The wire contract (`contracts.py` and `contract.ts`, held equal by the parity test) names every draft route, type, stream event and cap.

## Shaping

- **Starting.** `POST /drafts {need}` checks the need (1 to `NEED_MAX` characters), creates the Project named after the need (cut to `NAME_MAX` at a word boundary), makes it active, and stores the draft at `describe`. Nothing runs yet. The first `POST /drafts/{id}/say` with empty text runs the first stage turn with the need as the person's words. Later turns take what the person typed (`text`) or the suggestion they picked (`choice`, kept as their words), one or the other. The step becomes `shape`. Once the sketch has a `need` node, the Project takes that node's label, unless the person has already renamed it.
- **A design turn** is an ordinary turn in the draft's Project with the ledger surface `design`. It offers `recall`, `sketch` and `ask` only. Its frame carries the rubric, the sketch (ids, kind, slot, label, origin, confirmed), coverage, the questions asked per slot, and three rules: at most one question per reply, 2 to 4 short suggested answers, and never claim the sketch is complete. Tool parameters are strings, so a CLI engine can call them as OP lines. `sketch` takes `op, id, kind, slot, label, detail, link_from, link_to, quote`. `ask` takes `slot, text, suggestions`, with the suggestions joined by `" | "`. Outside a design turn both do nothing.
- **Sketch ops apply at once.** Each call is checked whole: a known kind and slot, a label of 1 to `LABEL_MAX` characters, at most `SKETCH_NODES_MAX` nodes, no duplicate ids, no dangling or duplicate links, and no node linked to itself. A bad call changes nothing, and the tool result tells the model why in plain words. Applied ops stream as `sketch{ops}`, each carrying the whole node after the change. Removing a node also removes its links, and each removed link is streamed as its own op. Nodes keep their insertion order, so a place never moves.
- **Origin is computed, not claimed.** A node the model adds is Athena's proposal (`origin: "athena"`, unconfirmed). There is one exception: the call carries a `quote` of 3 or more words that appears verbatim (case-insensitive, spaces collapsed) in one of the person's own messages in the draft, meaning the need, a typed line or a picked answer. Such a node is the person's (`origin: "you"`, confirmed). The person confirms proposals with `POST /drafts/{id}/confirm`, which keeps `origin: "athena"` and sets `confirmed: true`. The model may add a `detail` to the person's node but may not reword, re-slot or remove it; rewording a confirmed proposal makes it unconfirmed again, because the person agreed to the old words. Known limit: a quote proves the person said those words, not that the label means what they meant; the stage shows the person's nodes as theirs, so a wrong one is visible and the person can say so.
- **Questions are capped.** The first `ask` in a reply streams `ask{ask}` with `follow_up` set to the questions already asked on that slot. A second `ask` in the same reply is refused. So is one past `FOLLOW_UPS_MAX` follow-ups on a slot, with "you have asked enough about <slot>; move on or let the person write it".
- **Nothing is stored until the reply counts.** A reply counts when it has words, an applied op or a kept question. Only then are the sketch, the question counts and the step stored, followed by one `coverage{coverage, agreed}` event and `done`. An engine error ends with `error`, and so does a reply with no words, no op and no question (`engine_unavailable`, "She did not answer; try again."). In both cases the draft is left exactly as it was. Any `sketch` or `ask` events already streamed in that reply are void, and the stage reads the draft again. The person's line stays in the conversation, as it does on `/run`.
- **Skipping.** "That's enough, write it" moves every slot still at 0 or 0.5 to `skipped` (`Drafts.skip_open`). A skipped slot that later gains a node is no longer skipped, and a confirmed node always counts as 1.
- **One turn at a time.** `say`, `confirm` and `DELETE` answer 409 `space_busy` while a turn runs in the draft's Project, so a confirmation can never be overwritten by a reply that started before it. `DELETE /drafts/{id}` removes the draft's row and its folder, and the Project stays. Erasing the Project erases its drafts, rows and folders alike.
- **No key means a simulated stage.** The scripted engine first draws the need in the person's first words, then proposes one node for the next empty slot on each turn and asks about it. Every line it writes is labelled "(Simulated)". With the person's confirmations, a draft reaches agreed with no model.

## Authoring and the test run

- **Where a draft's chore lives.** `load_chore` requires `playbook.json` `id` to equal the folder name, so a written chore lives at `<data_dir>/drafts/<draft_id>/<chore_id>/`; erasing the draft still deletes `<data_dir>/drafts/<draft_id>/` whole. The loader checks `category` and `job` against the `taxonomy.json` beside the chore, else the app library's (`playbooks/taxonomy.json`), because the data folder has none; the library's own chores read exactly as before. Every step past shaping needs a data folder: a daemon with none answers 400.
- **Writing.** `POST /drafts/{id}/write` is taken at `shape` (or `author`, after a write that never finished) at any coverage: it first moves every open slot to `skipped` ("That's enough, write it"). The step becomes `author` and the stream opens with `writing`. One model call (surface `design`, no tools) is given the sketch by slot (each node marked as the person's words, a confirmed proposal or a guess; open slots named, to be filled with a stated default), the draft's conversation, the person's last `revise` note, the nearest library chore as an example (most shared words with the need and the sketch, the first on a tie; its `playbook.json` and `world.json` with every table cut to 3 rows, never its `truth.json`), the taxonomy ids and the rules the loader checks. It answers `{"playbook": {...}, "world": {...}}`, bare or fenced. Athena sets the `id`: a slug of the title (`[a-z0-9-]`, at most 80), unique across both libraries with `-2`, `-3`... It drops `featured`, writes the two files beside `truth.json` = `{"targets": [], "forbidden": []}`, and loads the folder.
- **Beyond the loader**, a written chore must have a phase, at least one act tool (any kind but `READ`), a param on every act tool, at most one param marked `"key": true` per act tool, and tool names a model can call (`[A-Za-z0-9_-]`, app and tool at most 64 characters together). The key param is the case a card is about; the loader keeps unknown param fields, so the mark rides in `world.json`, and a tool with no mark uses its first param.
- **Repairs.** Every problem (the loader's and these) is streamed as `repair{problems}` rewritten in plain words (the screen never names a file), and the model gets the original lines whole, with its own answer, and answers the whole object again. An answer with no readable JSON is a problem like any other. After `REPAIRS_MAX` repairs the stream ends `author_failed{problems}`, the half-written folder is cleared and the draft is back at `shape`. An engine error streams `error` and also goes back to `shape`. Success streams `written{chore_id}`, sets `chore_id`, clears any older `expected`, `key`, `bench`, `verdict` and `note`, and moves to `preview`.
- **The preview** (`POST /drafts/{id}/preview`, step `preview`) is one call given the written world (every table and tool, each act tool's key param) and the sketch. Each card must name an act tool of an app of the chore and a case; others are dropped, as is a second card for the same case (case-insensitive). Labels are cut at 160 characters and reasons at `DETAIL_MAX`, at most 40 cards, numbered `e1..en` and stored as the draft's `expected`. An answer with no usable card is `engine_unavailable`, never an empty list: an empty key would score every run as perfect.
- **The key** (`PUT /drafts/{id}/key`, at `preview` or again before the run) needs every expected card marked exactly once; an unknown id, a second mark or an unmarked card is a 400 that names it. Missing and never lines are trimmed, at most 20 of each and `DETAIL_MAX` characters each. When there are any, one call (surface `design`) places each missing line on an act tool and a case (`MissingCard.expected`, id `m<line>`) or leaves it as words, and names the act tool a never line plainly forbids. Marks alone need no model. `truth.json` is written from the key alone: one target per act tool and key param; yes marks and placed missing cards are eligible with `value_usd` 0 and the card's reason (the person's words for a missing card); no marks are traps with the person's note, else the card's reason; mapped never tools are forbidden. A case both eligible and a trap is eligible. A never line that names a tool the person also said yes to contradicts her marks and stays words. The chore is loaded again; if it no longer loads, the old `truth.json` is put back and the answer is a 400. The step becomes `rehearse`, and any older `bench` and `verdict` are cleared.
- **The test run** (`POST /drafts/{id}/rehearse`, step `rehearse`, a key set) runs the bench runner on a worker thread with the request's engine: a throwaway daemon, the written chore's phases, nothing approved. The stream reads the runner's events from a queue, so each goes out as it happens: `phase{index, app, message}` as each phase opens (`index` counts from 1) and `card{card}` as each portal card is filed (cards of the throwaway daemon, never on the person's gate). The draft's Project is held busy until the run ends, even when nobody is listening; the run then still finishes and its result is kept. Every model call of the run is copied into the real ledger with surface `rehearsal` and the draft's Project, stamped when copied. A run that ended early (an engine error, the 20 minute wall clock) is not a verdict: it streams `error` and the step stays `rehearse`. Otherwise `bench.json` is written beside the chore, the draft gets `bench`, the stream ends `scored{bench}` and the step becomes `verdict`. Nothing is retried automatically.
- **The verdict** (`POST /drafts/{id}/verdict`). `keep` is taken at `verdict` once a run is scored: the chore folder is copied to `<data_dir>/chores/<chore_id>/` (refused when either library already has that id), the Project's brief becomes the chore's promise (cut to `BRIEF_MAX`) and the step becomes `saved`. No app is added to the Project: a practice app's origin is invented, and a Project's apps are real addresses. `revise` is taken at `preview`, `rehearse` and `verdict`, so "this isn't what I meant" has a way back from each; the step becomes `shape`, the note is kept for the next write, and the sketch stays as it was.
- **Two libraries.** `GET /chores` lists the library (`origin: "library"`) and then the person's chores under `<data_dir>/chores/` (`origin: "yours"`). A chore of hers whose id the library has is skipped and logged, so ids never collide. `GET /chores/{id}` and a signed portal card (`turn._portal_for`) find a chore in either, the library first.
- **Without a real engine.** The scripted engine answers no authoring call with a chore, so on it a write ends `author_failed`. `python -m athena.drafts.demo --data <dir>` walks the staged need (its agreed sketch seeded, no shaping turns) through write, preview, a key of all yes and a test run on the engine `ATHENA_ENGINE` picks, printing each event and writing the whole record to `<dir>/runs/<stamp>-<chore id>/demo.json`.

## Consequences

- A need no library chore covers becomes a chore the person has tested against their own expectations, without a developer.
- A chore's score now depends on the key its owner gave. Two people with the same need can hold different keys, and both are right.
- `PUT` joins the daemon's allowed CORS methods for the key route.

## Amendment 2026-10-29 - two views of the stage, and the person's eraser

The stage's UI contest ended with two winners kept side by side: **Drawing** (the sketch fills the window as territories, the question is a note pinned below) and **Orbit** (the question card is the centre, the sketch circles it). The owner's verdict: "They will live together for a while, with more real use cases one will prove better, or we will develop both separately for different types of cases."

- **One model, two views.** Both views draw the same draft from one store and send the same actions. A tab switches between them, only one is mounted, and switching never moves a piece: each view keeps its own places by node id. The choice is kept per device.
- **The person's eraser.** `POST /drafts/{id}/drop {node_ids}` rubs out Athena's unconfirmed guesses and their links; the person's own words and anything inked are refused. With confirm, it is the person's half of pencil and ink: Athena draws, the person inks or erases. Undo is held on the desktop for a few seconds before the drop is sent, so the daemon never restores anything.
- **The evidence for choosing.** `SayBody.view` names the view the person answered in, and `Draft.views` counts the replies that counted per view (an error or an empty reply is not counted). With the verdict and the time to agreed, these counts are how a view proves itself on real needs; nothing else about the person is recorded.
- **A staged conversation in wire shape.** `scripts/stage_fixture.py` turns the contest's invented conversation into the exact streams and drafts the daemon answers, through the daemon's own sketch rules, into `apps/desktop/src/main/stage/fixture/staged.ts`; a test fails when it is out of date. The desktop's preview harness and the store's tests replay it with no daemon.
- **What the stage needs on the wire** (found by the shared stage's build, 2026-10-29): a person's node keeps the `quote` it was proved from, so the stage marks exactly those words in the person's sentence; the draft keeps the question still open (`Draft.ask`), so a reopened stage shows it; and a written chore's invented apps arrive as `PracticeApp {app_id, name, reads, acts, rows}` on `written` and on `Draft.practice`, so the stage names her practice apps and the test run's phases in plain words. The fixture's filed cards carry `{app, tool, params, chore}`, as the daemon files them.
